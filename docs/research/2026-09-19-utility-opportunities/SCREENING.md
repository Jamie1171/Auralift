# Screening decisions and reserve pile

Checked 19 September 2026. This records why attractive-looking ideas were narrowed or discarded. Depth varies deliberately: promising categories received review samples; obvious strong alternatives sometimes justified stopping at a listing or documentation check. “Reject” means do not prioritise this proposition now, not that the entire category can never support a business.

## Inventory: the strongest demotion

There is genuine value in knowing what is in a box, finding a tool and avoiding duplicate purchases. Positive reviews described moving, household organisation and large collections. But those needs are already served; their existence alone does not justify building.

| App | What it contributes to the comparison | What the sample suggested |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Itemtopia](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.itemtopia.app) | Flexible records, receipts and collaboration; premium beyond a small free allowance | Some current objections to limits and complexity; other users value the flexibility and willingly pay |
| [HouseBook](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=chenige.chkchk.wairz) | Nested storage, sharing, web access and exports; advertised one-time upgrade | Strong evidence against “all competitors force subscriptions”; some requests concern receipts and custom fields |
| [Magic Home Inventory](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=net.twisterrob.inventory) | Free local nested inventory with backup/import | Long-term positive use; manual-backup dependence and capture friction deserve attention |
| [StuffKeeper](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.stuffkeeper.au) | Visual nested organisation | Export, navigation and movement-history requests; an old annual-price complaint was answered by removing the subscription |
| [Memento](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.luckydroid.droidbase) | Highly configurable general database | Valuable flexibility; configuration effort and attachment portability can be difficult |
| [Find My Stuff](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.miquelcms.homeorganizer) | Offline organisation, photos, QR/barcodes, exports including images and backups | Strong current praise. Shared household use recurs as a request; the developer is already working on synchronisation |

The remaining hypothesis is **low-effort capture and collaborative movement of real boxes**, not another catalogue. However, [MOVINGBOXES](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=de.scalan.movingbox) already advertises helper sharing, labels and PDF reports; a July 2026 reviewer specifically praised sharing and reporting. [Moving Organizer Lite](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.theartofsignal.movingcompanion.lite) already separates a basic moving workflow from a paid version with additional sharing/export features. A vague “better moving app” is not enough.

Household collaboration also changes the economics. Reliable live multi-user sync, iPhone access, conflict handling and recovery are a service obligation. Passing around an archive is useful but is not equivalent to live collaboration. This would move the project away from the simple local utility Jamie initially wanted.

**Reopen only if** real movers show a repeated task the strongest alternatives handle poorly, and choose a prototype for that task even when the existing options are explained. Example to investigate: selecting items across several searches and moving them together into a new box. A single January 2026 Find My Stuff request supports investigating this; it does not prove a market.

For an eventual local inventory, photo recovery would be a core feature. Android cloud Auto Backup has a **25 MB per-app limit**, so it is not a complete photo-archive strategy. A portable archive needs copied attachments, versioned data, a tested restore and a user-controlled destination. [Android backup documentation](https://developer.android.com/identity/data/autobackup), [Storage Access Framework](https://developer.android.com/training/data-storage/shared/documents-files).

Other useful controls were the free [NAIC Home Inventory](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=org.naic), [Warranty Keeper](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.warranty_keeper) and the [HotStuff open-source project](https://github.com/PamCatten/hot-stuff). Their listings or project material were checked, not their on-device reliability. A [2023 community request](https://www.reddit.com/r/androidapps/comments/18tqzlm/some_kind_of_simple_home_inventory_app_i_dont/) ended with the requester finding StuffKeeper suitable and buying it—direct counterevidence to assuming every dissatisfied person remains unserved.

## Other important counterexamples

**Shift calendars:** [Supershift](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.supershift) and [My Shift Planner](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=uk.co.mybuzztechnologies.myShiftPlanner) had many positive reports about complex rotas and practical daily use. Supershift's [current help](https://supershift.app/help.html) documents a lifetime option; old cross-platform purchase complaints must not be treated as an unchanged current limitation. A specialised rota tool would need a particular occupation's unmet workflow, not just fewer ads.

**Subscription tracking:** [Tilla Premium](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.pavelrekun.tilla.premium) showed a **£1.99 UK upfront price** in the observed browser listing, with manual tracking, reminders and backup features. Its sample was largely positive. A local manual tracker with fair one-time pricing already exists.

**Craft counters:** [My Row Counter](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.row_counter) already combines counting with pattern handling and annotations. Several defects in its sampled reviews were followed by fixes or updated positive feedback. [knitCompanion](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.create2thrive.knitcompanion) is another sophisticated alternative. This category warrants a practising knitter's task comparison before any recommendation; one app's complaints are insufficient.

**Other free/cheap baselines:** [LocalSend](https://localsend.org/) supplies local cross-platform transfer; [OSS Document Scanner](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.akylas.documentscanner) exists for scanning; [Easy Voice Recorder Pro](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?gl=GB&hl=en-GB&id=com.digipom.easyvoicerecorder.pro) showed £4.59 in the checked UK listing. These checks prevented elevating generic copies. They were not exhaustive audits of those categories.

## Reasons to reopen a reserve

A new review cluster, a hands-on comparison, a narrowly defined professional task or a trusted distribution partner can change this ranking. “Another app has annoying ads” cannot. Record the new evidence, the strongest alternative and the reason a user would switch before promoting a reserve into the top three.
